R1, R2, R5, and R6

Case 31 Research Questions

Employees Federal Credit Union wanted to understand why members use the credit union, why they borrow elsewhere, how aware they are of available services, and how they evaluate operations.

  • R1: Why do people join the Credit Union?
  • R2: Why do members use other financial institutions when they need to borrow funds?
  • R5: How well are members aware of the services offered by the Credit Union?
  • R6: What are members’ attitudes and beliefs about how effectively the Credit Union is operated?

Research design and sample

  • Mail survey of current EFCU members
  • Simple random sample drawn from 3,531 members
  • 300 members selected for the original survey
  • Analysis file contains 128 respondents
  • Structured questions with Likert-type and categorical response scales

Analysis plan

Research question Measures Scale Descriptive analysis Inferential analysis
R1 Q7 savings rates 5-point Frequencies, mean One-sample t-test vs. 3
R2 Q8 borrowing rates; Q30 CU rates lower 5-point Frequencies, means One-sample t-tests vs. 3

R6 Q9-Q12; Q31-Q34; Q37 Mixed Frequencies, percentages, means Chi-square and one-sample t-tests

Descriptive patterns guide the interpretation. Test statistics appear only as supporting evidence.

R1 Savings rates are viewed as average

Members do not view the savings rate as meaningfully different from average.

M = 3.07, SD = 0.57; t(117) = 1.30, p = 0.196.

R2 Borrowing rates do not provide a clear advantage

R2 Interpretation

Measure Valid n Mean SD Test
Q8: borrowing rates 107 3.14 0.76 t(106) = 1.91, p = 0.058
Q30: CU rates lower 128 3.06 0.87 t(127) = 0.82, p = 0.416

Members see borrowing rates as approximately average and do not clearly agree that EFCU offers lower rates than competitors.

This weak perceived rate advantage helps explain why some members borrow from other financial institutions.

R5 Awareness varies sharply by service

R5 Interpretation

Finding Result
Highest unawareness Regular share account: 64.8% unaware
Highest reported use MasterCard credit card: 43.8% used
Strong awareness, limited use Signature loans: 81.2% aware but not used
Services needing attention IRA loans and share-collateralized loans

Awareness patterns differed across all services.

All fourteen chi-square goodness-of-fit tests were significant at p < .001. Responses were concentrated in particular awareness levels.

Priority: promote high-unawareness services and examine why loan awareness does not translate into use.

R6 Account communication is mostly effective

77.3% receive statements about as often as they want, while 22.7% say statements are not frequent enough. Accuracy ratings are strongly favorable: 95.3% selected Good or Excellent.

R6 Confidentiality and service delivery

Statements are easy to understand, while confidence in confidentiality is less universal.

Easy to understand: 97.7% Yes (chi-square(1) = 116.28, p < .001).

Information kept confidential: 69.8% Yes among valid responses (chi-square(1) = 16.64, p < .001).

R6 Operations receive positive ratings

Each mean was significantly above the neutral benchmark of 3 (all p < .001). Overall management and operations received the strongest rating (M = 4.07).

Conclusions

  1. Savings and borrowing rates lack a clear advantage. Members rate both near average, and they do not clearly agree that EFCU loan rates are lower than competitors.
  2. Service awareness is uneven. Awareness varies greatly by service, and several loan products are known but not used.
  3. Operational perceptions are favorable. Members report clear statements, accurate information, prompt service, and positive overall management.
  4. Communication still has gaps. About one-quarter want statements more frequently, and confidentiality receives a weaker favorable percentage than statement clarity.
